# Kuymo — Marketing Plan v1

**Prepared for:** MostafaSensei / Kuymo  
**Date:** 26 November 2026  
**Status:** Draft v1 for owner review; not an approved campaign or a growth forecast.  
**Resources:** One developer, $0/month marketing allocation, 2–3 hours/week.  
**Product:** Free, open-source Android video compression. Google Play is coming soon.  
**Context:** [Product positioning](product-marketing-context.md) · [Evidence and open questions](research.md).

## 1. Executive summary

Kuymo should win its first users by making one everyday job understandable: prepare a video for sharing when its file is too large. Lead with a size-first workflow, show real interface screenshots, and explain the quality trade-off instead of promising magical compression. The product has an independent voice and a clear local-processing story; neither requires invented download counts or a large paid acquisition machine.

The immediate constraint is public availability and evidence, not reach at any cost. A visitor should understand what Kuymo does, see the current interface, learn that Google Play is not yet available, and have a straightforward way to follow published releases. After the listing is verified, the same journey can lead to the official store. Before that point a disabled store badge or a misleading download button would spend trust that the project has not earned.

**Three ranked bets:**

1. **A clear store-to-product story.** Make the website, store description and eventual screenshots answer the same question: “How do I choose a size for this video?” This serves Acquisition and Activation. Completion means a truthful listing, consistent language and a tested mobile path, not a traffic forecast.
2. **A reusable demonstration asset.** Make one real, short explanation of the workflow and reuse its frames in store screenshots, a product post and a help answer. Today only welcome-screen captures are available. Add the owner's full screenshots before presenting other screens as pictured. This serves Acquisition and Activation while keeping the workload small.
3. **A tight feedback-to-improvement loop.** Invite a few relevant Android users to try the actual task, observe confusion, fix the biggest issue, and publish a concise change note. This serves Activation, Retention and Referral. The first output is a better task outcome and useful language, not an impressive follower count.

The first 90 days should establish a credible product surface and a sustainable weekly routine. The twelve-month aim is an evidence-led, maintainable organic channel around one useful job, with enough user feedback to decide what deserves more time. No numeric install, ranking, retention or revenue forecast is defensible with the current data. Set a baseline when publication occurs and review attainable targets after four weeks of observations.

## 2. Strategic frame

**Category claim:** A target-size video compressor for Android. **Audience:** ordinary people sharing a video, with privacy concerns or frustration around file limits. Creators and storage-focused users are secondary; do not turn the homepage into a catalog of every possible video workflow.

**Promise:** Choose a target size, review the plan, process locally, inspect the result, then save or share. **Boundary:** smaller files may require lower quality or resolution, and not every source can meet every cap. Presets are starting points, not guarantees about another platform's changing limits.

**Business model:** Free and open source at launch. There is no subscription funnel, paid upgrade or ad revenue target in this plan. GPL licensing does not itself create a donation or sponsorship program; none is advertised. The sustainability resource is the founder's time.

**Positioning ladder:**

| Visitor question       | Message                                              | Supporting surface                                    |
| ---------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Why would I need this? | A video can be too large to share                    | Homepage introduction / store short description       |
| What do I do?          | Start with a size, inspect the plan                  | Feature explanation and eventual workflow screenshots |
| Can I trust it?        | Encoding runs on your device; trade-offs are visible | Privacy explanation, source link, candid FAQ          |
| Can I get it?          | Coming soon; follow releases until publication       | Central launch state / download page                  |

Voice is calm, capable and direct. The Retro Japanese frame gives the project personality; it must not make instructions cryptic. English and Arabic are the maintained website languages. Other application languages are not silently advertised as published website locales. Do not use the Japanese motif to imply a Japanese founder or a translation of the name.

## 4. Acquisition — strangers become aware

**Primary surface:** the eventual Google Play listing and a clear product website. **One active distribution lane:** founder-led helpful explanations, in the language/community where the founder can answer questions. English is the reusable source asset; Arabic gets a natural companion version when time permits. Do not create seven social accounts to cross-post the same link.

Before launch, finish the website and listing assets, clarify availability, and make one concise explanation of target size versus quality. Share it only in a relevant space that permits product posts, with an honest note that the app is not yet on Google Play. Respond to the actual problem rather than dropping a promotional link into every discussion.

At publication, use a store title/short description that describes the job, then captions on screenshots that explain the workflow. Follow current Play Console rules at upload time; this plan does not assume a fixed screenshot count or guarantee ranking. The website should use the same wording and a verified store link. A release note can become one post and one support answer, rather than three new content projects.

**First 90 days:** ship the asset foundation, choose one community/channel, post at most one useful demonstration per week, and review what questions repeat. **Months 4–12:** publish a focused help page only when a real repeated question exists. Add a comparison only after verifying another product's actual behavior. Do not scale thin programmatic pages or buy backlinks.

**Tools:** Astro website, existing screenshot manifest, Google Play Console after access/publication, Search Console after a real domain is configured, GitHub releases/issues. **Skills:** landing-page-generator, product-marketing-context, ux-copy and marketing-psychology. No account connection, campaign or external message was executed by this work.

## 5. Activation — first useful outcome

For this product activation is not signup: there is no required account flow. The intended first useful outcome is a completed compression whose result the user can inspect and save/share. This is a product-value definition, not an existing analytics event.

The website reduces pre-install uncertainty through actual screens, a short three-step explanation and honest answers about size and quality. The current gallery shows only the welcome screen; it should say that openly. When the owner supplies workflow captures, replace them through the manifest with accurate captions. A simulated planner is explicitly labelled as an illustration and is not used as proof of compression performance.

Run small voluntary usability sessions after a build is available. Ask a participant to prepare their own non-sensitive sample or a supplied test clip for a particular sharing need. Observe what they think the target means, whether they understand adjustments, and whether they can find save/share. Never ask people to upload private media for marketing research. Record the task, confusion and outcome, not the video.

Two or three focused sessions can reveal an obvious misunderstanding; they cannot establish a conversion rate or population-level quality benchmark. Fix the highest-impact problem, repeat the task with a new participant, and reflect the language back into the website. Pause content creation if the core task is unreliable.

**Tools/skills:** manual notes, issue templates, existing app tests; ux-design-from-scratch, ui-review, ux-feedback. Runtime event instrumentation is a separate product/privacy decision, not silently included in the website.

## 6. Retention — being useful again

This is an episodic utility. A person may need to compress a video occasionally, so daily active use is a poor default success target. Do not send repetitive reminders to manufacture app opens. Reliable saved results, understandable errors and a predictable next session are more useful than a lifecycle notification campaign.

Maintain concise release notes, triage issues weekly, and prioritize repeat friction: inaccessible saved output, confusing settings, unsupported media explanations or interrupted jobs. Publish a short explanation when a fix addresses a common problem. If someone returns with another video, the interface should still be familiar.

Measure observed repeat use only when there is an authorized, privacy-appropriate source. Without that, track recurring issue categories and voluntary follow-up feedback. A decline in support questions is not automatically a retention improvement: it could mean fewer users. Record uncertainty.

**Now:** support and changelog hygiene. **Later:** optional, owner-approved feedback collection if the manual loop cannot answer an important question. **Skipped:** email sequences, push reactivation, win-back offers and subscriptions.

## 7. Referral — useful recommendations

The natural referral is a person who successfully prepared a video telling someone facing the same problem. Make the app/site identity and release URL easy to share; do not watermark personal videos with marketing material or require a referral to unlock compression.

After a real positive task outcome, a voluntary invitation to share feedback is reasonable. Any future in-app review request must follow the platform's rules, remain optional and never gate support. No review prompt is implemented in the website work. Do not offer rewards for positive ratings, suppress critical users or invent testimonials before receiving permission to quote a real person.

For the first quarter, prefer permission-based qualitative feedback over a referral scheme. In later quarters, assess whether genuine recommendations bring the same type of user. If not observable, keep it as a hypothesis. The project does not need an affiliate system at zero revenue and zero budget.

**How to start / outcome / risk:**

- Listing: align the short description, three-step story and accurate screenshots. Expected outcome is clearer comprehension; traffic impact is unproven. Risk is polishing assets while ignoring task failures. Use only actual availability and capability claims.
- Demo: record one complete task with a permitted sample after the owner supplies the missing views. Reuse frames and captions. Expected outcome is a better explanation, not a guaranteed conversion lift. Risk is stale UI; refresh when behavior changes.
- Observations: invite a small number of willing users, take notes without collecting their media, and fix the most consequential confusion. Expected outcome is actionable usability evidence. Risk is over-generalizing a tiny sample.
- Community: choose one relevant space, read its rules and answer real questions before sharing a project link. Expected outcome is relevant conversation and vocabulary. Risk is spam or spending the weekly time budget on promotion alone.
- Help page: write only after repeated demand, keep the answer specific and link to the relevant feature. Expected outcome is self-service clarity. Risk is thin SEO content; stop if it cannot answer a real question.

## 13. Measurement, RACI and open decisions

**Candidate north star:** a useful compression completed and saved/shared, evaluated in aggregate only if an authorized measurement source exists. It is not the same as app opens, downloads, or the demonstration slider on the website.

| Stage                 | Leading indicator                                               | Current source/status                                       |
| --------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Acquisition           | Store visitors, search impressions, relevant referral questions | Play/Search Console after setup; currently unknown          |
| Website consideration | Can a visitor explain the target-size workflow?                 | Voluntary comprehension observation; no site click tracking |
| Activation            | Can a first-time user complete and inspect the task?            | Small manual sessions; instrumented rate unavailable        |
| Retention             | Does the app help when another video needs sharing?             | Voluntary follow-up; no validated retention cohorts         |
| Referral              | Users voluntarily recommend the project                         | Permission-based comments/notes; attribution unknown        |
| Sustainability        | Weekly time used and recurring support cost                     | Founder time log; cash marketing allocation remains zero    |

Do not derive a gallery click-through rate without an event source. Do not call store installs active users. Do not compare prelaunch followers with post-launch retention. Establish a baseline after publication, then change one important message at a time. With low traffic, a short usability observation is often more informative than a statistically weak A/B test.

**RACI:** MostafaSensei is accountable and responsible for release verification, public claims, content, support and measurement decisions. Volunteer testers are consulted only with consent. Visitors are informed through accurate copy. There is no assumed designer, marketer, agency or paid translator. Native-speaker review remains a quality need, not a resource already secured.

**Open decisions:** production domain; Play launch date/status; final workflow screenshots; release-validated AI behavior; native-speaker review; actual store access and baseline metrics; whether any future privacy-preserving measurement is worth implementing; ongoing engineering/hosting costs. No funding date, store ranking, CAC, LTV or revenue projection has been invented.

**Review cadence:** 20-minute weekly evidence review within the time box; one 45-minute quarterly review replacing that week's content production. Update the plan when availability or resources change. The thirteen sections are drafted for review, not silently approved. Revisions should keep the owner inputs and evidence record together.
